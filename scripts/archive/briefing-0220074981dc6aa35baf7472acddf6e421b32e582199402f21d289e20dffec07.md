### Summary\nThe article outlines a significant transformation of the internet's advertising landscape driven by autonomous AI agents. These agents will become primary internet users, creating an \"agentic Web\" and an \"agent attention economy\" where advertisers compete to influence agent decisions via protocols like Anthropic's MCP and Google's A2A. This shift mandates an evolution from traditional SEO to agent-focused optimization, potentially involving dense vector representations and an \"agentic PageRank\" based on utility. While human oversight through trusted subscriptions is envisioned, substantial challenges in technology development and user trust must be overcome, leading to a conventional web increasingly tailored for agent interactions.\n\n### Why It Matters\nThis impending shift from human-centric to agent-centric internet interaction represents a profound paradigm change for AI professionals. It signals a future where AI isn't merely a tool *for* humans, but an autonomous participant and primary economic actor. For researchers, this opens vast new frontiers in designing robust multi-agent systems, modeling complex AI-driven economic behaviors, and developing ethical frameworks to govern agent autonomy, bias, and transparency in decision-making. AI engineers and product developers face the immediate challenge of designing interfaces and protocols—like Google's A2A and Anthropic's MCP—that facilitate secure, efficient, and transparent agent-to-agent and agent-to-human interactions. This will spawn entirely new market segments focused on \"agent services\" and agent-centric infrastructure. Data scientists will encounter novel challenges in analyzing agent behavior, optimizing content for machine consumption, and defining metrics for \"agent attention,\" requiring advanced techniques for vector-based optimization and agentic PageRank algorithms. Ultimately, those AI professionals who anticipate, understand, and strategically build for this \"agentic Web\" will be at the forefront of defining the next era of digital commerce, interaction, and the very architecture of the internet itself. The implications extend beyond technology, demanding proactive engagement with regulatory bodies to ensure fairness, transparency, and user control in this evolving digital economy.
