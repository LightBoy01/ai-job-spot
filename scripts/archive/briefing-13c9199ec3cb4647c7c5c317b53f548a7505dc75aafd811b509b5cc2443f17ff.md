### Summary\nThis research presents a groundbreaking demonstration of Large AI Models (LAMs), specifically a LoRA-fine-tuned LLAMA-class model, achieving the first standards-compliant emulation of the Radio Resource Control (RRC) layer in mobile networks. By treating RRC as a domain-specific language and creating a novel question-answer dataset from real 4G and 5G network traces, which preserves Abstract Syntax Notation (ASN.1) structure, the 8B parameter model successfully orchestrates control-plane procedures. It achieved a median cosine similarity of 0.97 and high conformance rates, a 61% relative gain over zero-shot baselines, laying a critical foundation for future AI-native Radio Access Networks (RAN) and 6G protocol intelligence.\n\n### Why It Matters\nThis work is a pivotal moment for the convergence of AI and telecommunications, signifying a leap towards truly \"AI-Native\" 6G networks where intelligence scales beyond traditional handcrafted protocols. For AI professionals, this research showcases a powerful and practical application of Large Language Models (LLMs) in a highly structured, performance-critical, and previously human-logic-dominated engineering domain. It demonstrates that LLMs can move beyond conversational AI to understand, interpret, and generate complex, formal network protocols, effectively acting as sophisticated control-plane agents. This breakthrough promises more adaptive, resilient, and self-optimizing RANs, capable of dynamic resource management, automated fault recovery, and real-time network optimization, which are crucial for the demanding requirements of 6G. Furthermore, the methodology of constructing a \"segmentation-safe, question-answer dataset\" that meticulously preserves ASN.1 structure offers a transferable blueprint for integrating LLMs into other intricate, domain-specific technical systems, highlighting a path for AI to automate and enhance complex industrial and infrastructure operations well beyond its current applications. It's a clear signal that general-purpose AI is becoming capable of mastering highly specialized, mission-critical tasks.
