### Summary\\nThis research introduces a groundbreaking AI methodology using score-based models for strong gravitational lensing analysis. It uniquely enables the joint inference of both background galaxies (sources) and the foreground lens's mass distribution without prior knowledge of the lens mass – a significant departure from previous methods. Employing a continuous-time sampler based on GibbsDDRM, the approach successfully reconstructs source and lens parameters with high accuracy and unbiased recovery, demonstrating the first successful \\\"blind\\\" inference of this type using score-based priors in astrophysics.\\n\\n### Why It Matters\\nThis development represents a significant leap forward for AI in scientific discovery, particularly for professionals in the AI space. It underscores a pivotal shift where AI transcends its role as a mere analytical tool to become an autonomous engine for fundamental research, tackling some of the most complex inverse problems in science.\\n\\nFirstly, this work dramatically advances the \\\"AI for Science\\\" paradigm. By enabling \\\"blind\\\" inference – reconstructing unknown parameters without pre-existing assumptions about the foreground lens – score-based generative models are proving indispensable in domains where data is scarce, noisy, or incomplete. This capability moves AI from hypothesis testing to hypothesis generation, allowing for genuine, data-driven discovery that might elude human intuition or traditional computational methods. For AI developers, it highlights the immense potential of generative models, beyond image or text generation, as powerful data-driven priors capable of unlocking hidden information within complex datasets.\\n\\nSecondly, the successful, unbiased recovery of true lens parameters is crucial for building trust in AI-driven scientific insights. In high-stakes fields like astrophysics, where discoveries can reshape our understanding of the universe, the reliability and interpretability of AI models are paramount. This research demonstrates that sophisticated AI can deliver results not only consistent with observational noise but also free from systematic bias, bolstering confidence in its application for fundamental research, especially as instruments like JWST and Euclid generate unprecedented volumes of data. This also provides a compelling case study for the generalizability of robust AI architectures to problems with intricate physics.\\n\\nFinally, the implications stretch far beyond astrophysics. The methodology pioneered here for joint, \\\"blind\\\" inference in gravitational lensing offers a blueprint for addressing inverse problems across a myriad of scientific and engineering disciplines. Imagine similar breakthroughs in medical imaging, where AI could autonomously reconstruct internal structures from incomplete scans without explicit models, or in materials science, to infer atomic arrangements from noisy diffraction patterns. This research showcases AI's potential to fundamentally transform how we interpret data and accelerate discovery across the entire scientific spectrum, cementing its role as an indispensable partner in pushing the boundaries of human knowledge.
