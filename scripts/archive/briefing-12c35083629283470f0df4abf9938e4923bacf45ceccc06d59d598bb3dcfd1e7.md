### Summary\nThis research introduces a novel clustered multitask approach to address adversarially robust adaptive control in linear quadratic regulator (LQR) tasks. The proposed method enables multiple systems to collaboratively learn control policies even in the presence of model uncertainty and malicious corruption. By integrating clustering, system identification, and resilient aggregation, the approach effectively mitigates the impact of corrupted model updates. The analysis demonstrates that this strategy reduces control regret inversely with the number of honest systems in a cluster, a benefit that holds true even when a bounded fraction of systems within each cluster are adversarial.\n\n### Why It Matters\nThis research is crucial for AI professionals, particularly those involved in developing and deploying collaborative autonomous systems, such as fleets of drones, self-driving cars, or industrial robots. As AI systems become increasingly interconnected and rely on shared learning, their vulnerability to adversarial attacks and data corruption escalates. This work offers a practical framework for building inherently more resilient AI control systems. The ability to maintain performance and reduce regret even with a bounded fraction of adversarial agents within a cluster means that system designers can architect robust systems without needing perfect trust assumptions for every single agent. This translates to more reliable and safer deployments in real-world, potentially hostile environments, reducing risks associated with system failures or malicious manipulation. Furthermore, the focus on non-asymptotic bounds provides tangible performance guarantees, moving beyond theoretical possibilities to quantifiable real-world applicability. This study pushes the boundaries of trustworthy AI, enabling the development of robust, adaptable, and secure multi-agent systems critical for future AI-powered infrastructure.
