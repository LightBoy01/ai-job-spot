### Summary\nThis study investigates and proposes solutions for taxonomic inconsistencies within Wikidata, the world's largest open knowledge graph. Attributing these issues to its open-editing nature, the research introduces a novel validation method to pinpoint classification errors, over-generalized subclass links, and redundant connections in specific domains. It further presents a new criterion to assess the urgency of corrections and develops a user-facing system designed to harness Wikidata's crowdsourcing capabilities for enhancing the precision of its taxonomic relationships.\n\n### Why It Matters\nFor AI professionals, the semantic integrity of knowledge graphs like Wikidata is paramount. These graphs serve as the bedrock for a vast array of AI applications, from powering sophisticated search engines and intelligent assistants to enabling explainable AI and training large language models. When foundational data within these graphs contains inconsistencies—such as incorrect classifications or redundant links—AI systems built upon them inherently absorb and propagate these inaccuracies. This can lead to flawed reasoning, biased outputs, and a profound erosion of trust in AI-driven insights. This research directly addresses the critical \"garbage in, garbage out\" challenge, highlighting that the pursuit of more advanced AI models is futile without a simultaneous commitment to impeccable data quality. The proposed crowdsourced validation system offers a scalable, community-driven model for continuous data curation, essential for maintaining the reliability and trustworthiness of the knowledge bases that fuel the global AI ecosystem. Ultimately, initiatives like this are not merely about database maintenance; they are about laying a solid, dependable foundation for the future of ethical, accurate, and impactful artificial intelligence.
