### Summary
This briefing highlights a critical paradox in the AI industry: the increasing energy demands of advanced AI technologies, particularly large data centers, are inadvertently contributing to the continued reliance on and operation of coal-fired power plants. Despite AI representing the \"technology of the future,\" its substantial power consumption is extending the lifespan of \"energy of the past,\" specifically coal, raising significant concerns about AI's hidden environmental footprint.

### Why It Matters
This news is a wake-up call for AI professionals, from researchers to business leaders, to confront the often-overlooked environmental cost of their innovation. The rapid expansion of AI, particularly the training and deployment of large models, is creating an insatiable demand for electricity. When this demand is met by legacy fossil fuel infrastructure, it directly undermines global climate goals and the very sustainability narratives many AI applications aim to support. For AI companies, this isn't just an abstract environmental concern; it represents a significant reputational risk, potential future regulatory burdens (e.g., carbon taxes, reporting requirements), and a critical challenge to long-term operational sustainability. Professionals in the AI space must prioritize 'green AI' not just as a buzzword, but as an engineering imperative - demanding more energy-efficient algorithms, hardware, and a proactive shift towards renewable energy sources for data centers. The ethical implications are profound: if the 'technology of the future' inadvertently props up the most polluting 'energy of the past,' AI's promise to humanity becomes deeply compromised.
