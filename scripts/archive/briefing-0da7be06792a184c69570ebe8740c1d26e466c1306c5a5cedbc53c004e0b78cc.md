### Summary\nThe TRADE conducted an interview with Nicholas Phillips, a market structure research analyst at Bloomberg Intelligence (BI), following the release of BI's 'European institutional equity trading study 2025' report. The discussion centered on Europe's re-energized trading momentum, projections for 2026, and evolving dynamics in post-trade operations, dark trading caps, and the growth of bilateral trading.\n\n### Why It Matters\nWhile the briefing doesn't explicitly mention artificial intelligence, the topics discussed are highly pertinent to AI professionals in the financial sector. Discussions around \"renewed trading momentum\" and \"what to look out for in 2026\" underscore the constant need for sophisticated predictive analytics and real-time insights, areas where AI and machine learning models are becoming indispensable for identifying trends and informing trading strategies. Furthermore, \"post-trade developments,\" \"dark trading caps,\" and \"rising bilateral\" trading represent complex, data-intensive challenges ripe for AI-driven solutions. AI can significantly enhance efficiency and reduce operational risk in post-trade reconciliation, optimize execution strategies within dark pools while adhering to regulatory caps, and manage the growing complexity and data volume associated with increased bilateral trading. For AI professionals, this signals a clear demand for intelligent automation, advanced algorithmic trading systems, and robust compliance monitoring tools powered by AI, highlighting the ongoing technological transformation of global financial market infrastructure. Ignoring these market structure shifts would mean missing critical opportunities to apply AI where it can deliver substantial value and competitive advantage.
