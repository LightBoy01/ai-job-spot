### Summary\\nThis paper introduces Progressive Inference-Time Annealing (PITA), a novel framework designed to efficiently sample from target unnormalized probability densities, a critical challenge across scientific domains. PITA addresses the scalability limitations of existing diffusion-based samplers by ingeniously combining two interpolation techniques: Boltzmann distribution annealing and diffusion smoothing. The method trains a sequence of diffusion models from high to low temperatures, leveraging progressively higher-temperature training with engineered sample access. Crucially, PITA uses inference-time annealing via a novel Feynman-Kac PDE combined with Sequential Monte Carlo to generate training samples for the subsequent, lower-temperature diffusion models. This innovative approach empirically enables, for the first time, equilibrium sampling of complex molecular systems, including N-body particle systems, Alanine Dipeptide, and tripeptides in Cartesian coordinates, with dramatically fewer energy function evaluations than previous methods.\\n\\n### Why It Matters\\nThe development of PITA represents a significant leap forward in AI's application to fundamental scientific challenges, particularly in computational chemistry and physics. For professionals in the AI space, this work underscores the immense potential of generative AI, specifically diffusion models, to transcend their common applications in image or text generation and tackle long-standing problems in scientific discovery. The \"first-time\" achievement of equilibrium sampling for complex molecular systems like Alanine Dipeptide is not merely an incremental improvement; it signifies a breakthrough that could profoundly accelerate research in drug discovery, materials science, and biochemistry. By dramatically reducing the number of energy function evaluations, PITA makes previously intractable or prohibitively expensive simulations feasible, enabling deeper understanding of molecular behaviors and facilitating the design of new molecules with desired properties.\\n\\nThis innovation highlights a crucial underlying trend: the convergence of advanced AI methodologies with core scientific disciplines to solve foundational problems. It demonstrates how understanding physical principles (like Boltzmann distributions and temperature annealing) can be meticulously integrated with cutting-edge machine learning techniques to create powerful new tools. For AI developers, PITA offers inspiration for innovative training paradigms (sequential, progressive training) and the integration of mathematical physics (Feynman-Kac PDE) into machine learning frameworks. Furthermore, it points to a burgeoning area for AI investment and talent—AI for Science—where the impact extends beyond commercial gains to fundamental human knowledge and technological advancement. This work positions AI not just as a data analysis tool, but as a direct simulator and accelerator for the scientific method itself, opening doors to discoveries that were once out of reach.
