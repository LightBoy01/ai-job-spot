### Summary
This briefing highlights the significant challenges faced by machine learning, particularly generative models, when attempting to solve \"very hard problems\" such as theorem proving, algorithmic design, and drug discovery. While the standard approach involves pre-training followed by post-training with scalar reward signals, two major hurdles emerge: reward sparsity, where positive outcomes are so rare that models struggle to find them, and costly reward evaluation, where determining the quality of a generated sample is expensive or risky, making extensive trial-and-error impractical. This predicament limits the ability of powerful models like a hypothetical GPT-5 to make breakthroughs in areas requiring extensive, intelligent exploration without clear positive feedback.

### Why It Matters
This analysis underscores a fundamental bottleneck in AI's progression towards truly autonomous scientific discovery and complex problem-solving. For AI professionals, it's crucial to recognize that simply scaling up model size or training data won't inherently overcome the challenges of sparse rewards and costly evaluations in high-stakes domains. This problem isn't just about niche applications; it points to a critical limitation of current reward-centric learning paradigms when facing open-ended, real-world problems where success is rare and experimentation is prohibitive. The inability to efficiently explore and learn in such environments means that AI systems struggle to move beyond interpolation to true innovation. The underlying trend is a growing need for novel exploration strategies, moving beyond sole reliance on positive reinforcement to effectively leverage negative signals, intrinsic curiosity, and sample-efficient learning. Overcoming this requires a paradigm shift towards algorithms that can intelligently navigate vast, unrewarding search spaces, learn from partial information or failures, and make progress even when explicit positive feedback is virtually non-existent, paving the way for AI to genuinely push human limits in scientific and intellectual endeavors.
