### Summary
The author proposes \"Humanoid Olympic Events\" to challenge roboticists, critiquing current robot competitions for lacking real-world applicability and focusing on spectacle over utility. He identifies key limitations in current robotic manipulation, which primarily relies on learning from human demonstration via puppeteering. These limitations include a lack of high-resolution force feedback, restricted finger control, the absence of robust touch sensing, and only medium precision (1-3 cm). His proposed Olympics feature five categories-Doors, Laundry, Tools, Fingertip Manipulation, and Wet Manipulation-each with bronze, silver, and gold medal tasks. These challenges are meticulously designed to push the boundaries of robotic dexterity, whole-body coordination, and robustness to achieve truly general-purpose capabilities essential for navigating and performing tasks in complex human environments.

### Why It Matters
This initiative is critical for the AI industry as it clearly articulates and defines the next frontier for general-purpose humanoid robots, moving beyond controlled laboratory demonstrations to real-world, messy utility. For professionals in AI, robotics, and product development, it highlights that while current AI and machine learning techniques excel in pattern recognition and specific, well-defined tasks, they are still fundamentally limited in physical interaction. Achieving these \"Olympic\" tasks demands significant innovation in embodied AI, requiring breakthroughs in areas such as high-fidelity tactile sensing, precise force control, adaptive full-body coordination, and robust handling of deformable and wet objects. This pragmatic roadmap provides concrete, measurable goals that will directly drive R&D, focusing investment on capabilities that unlock immense market potential across domestic assistance, elder care, logistics, and complex service industries. Overcoming these challenges will not only accelerate the deployment of truly versatile robots but also necessitate deeper integration of perception, cognition, and fine motor control, fundamentally shaping the economic and societal impact of future AI systems. It serves as a vital call to action for the robotics community to develop more robust, dexterous, and adaptable AI, capable of navigating the unpredictable complexities of human environments.
