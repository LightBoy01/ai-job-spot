<p>A few days back, I published a list of 650+ frontend interview questions.</p>

<p>That post got attention, feedback, and a surprising amount of messages from developers (both junior and senior) telling me it helped them prep better.</p>

<p>But something didn't feel complete.</p>

<p>Because questions alone are not enough.</p>

<p>So I continued learning deeply, exploring each topic, facing more interviews, reading docs, breaking code, and then documenting the patterns I found.</p>

<p>And that small side note list turned into something else completely.</p>

<h2>
  
  
  What's new in v2.0?
</h2>

<ul>
<li>1,020+ questions with detailed answers</li>
<li>185+ coding problems (each solved step-by-step)</li>
<li>600+ flashcards for fast revision</li>
<li>new sections: TypeScript, Security, Testing, Tooling</li>
<li>difficulty tags, interview frequency, company tags</li>
<li>code examples + common mistakes + time to answer estimates</li>
</ul>

<p>This is no longer "a question bank".<br>
This is a <strong>complete frontend mastery system</strong>.</p>

<h2>
  
  
  Why this matters
</h2>

<p>Most interview prep content online is too shallow.</p>

<p>It teaches the <em>what</em>.</p>

<p>But senior interviews check the <em>why</em>.</p>

<p>Why does React re-render?<br>
Why does this promise resolve later?<br>
Why Next.js instead of client-side React?</p>

<p>This project grew because I kept asking that exact question myself.</p>
