### Summary\nThis paper introduces a novel 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) algorithm to model wideband radio frequency (RF) radiance fields, addressing the limitation of existing 3DGS methods which only reconstruct fields at single frequencies. The proposed model incorporates a frequency-embedded electromagnetic (EM) feature network that uses 3D Gaussian spheres to learn the relationship between frequency and RF transmission characteristics within a given spatial environment. Trained on sparse frequency samples, it can efficiently reconstruct RF fields at arbitrary and unseen frequencies across a wide range (1-94 GHz). Evaluated on a new large-scale Power Angular Spectrum (PAS) dataset, the model significantly outperforms single-frequency approaches, achieving an SSIM of 0.922, paving the way for advanced applications like heterogeneous RF system deployment and cross-band communication.\n\n### Why It Matters\nThis research represents a significant leap for professionals in the AI and wireless communications fields, moving beyond static, single-frequency understandings of radio environments towards dynamic, wideband modeling. For AI professionals, it underscores the growing trend of integrating advanced neural rendering techniques like 3D Gaussian Splatting with complex physical phenomena, extending AI's capabilities into the invisible electromagnetic spectrum. This convergence allows for the creation of sophisticated \"digital twins\" of wireless spaces, enabling highly accurate predictions of signal propagation, attenuation, and interference across diverse frequencies from narrowband IoT to millimeter-wave. Such a capability is foundational for optimizing future wireless networks, especially with the advent of 6G and beyond, where heterogeneous RF systems and dynamic spectrum management will be paramount. It means AI can now empower engineers to design and deploy wireless infrastructure with unprecedented precision, leading to more efficient spectrum utilization, reduced energy consumption, and enhanced network reliability. Furthermore, this breakthrough unlocks a new frontier for applications in spatial AI, enabling next-generation distributed RF sensing for security or health monitoring, highly reliable cross-band communication systems, and and even advancements in electromagnetic cloaking or smart surfaces. Ultimately, this work is not merely an incremental improvement; it's a foundational piece for building intelligent, adaptable, and highly efficient wireless ecosystems, profoundly impacting everything from smart cities and industrial IoT to advanced robotics and augmented reality.
