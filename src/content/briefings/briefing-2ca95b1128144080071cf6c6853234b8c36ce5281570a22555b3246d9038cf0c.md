### Summary\nThe article employs the classic \"faster horses\" analogy to critique the prevailing incremental approach to customer service improvements. It argues that merely optimizing existing, failing systems is insufficient for true transformation. Instead, it advocates for a radical shift, implying that new paradigms and technologies—much like the car to the horse—are necessary to fundamentally reinvent customer experience rather than just making current methods marginally better.\n\n### Why It Matters\nFor professionals in the AI space, this article serves as a critical strategic compass, highlighting not just an opportunity, but an imperative to think disruptively. It challenges the common pitfall of applying AI to automate or incrementally improve broken processes, which effectively translates to building \"faster horses.\" Instead, it urges AI architects, data scientists, and strategists to envision \"cars\"—entirely new solutions that reimagine the foundational interactions and operations within customer service.\n\nThis perspective is vital because it underscores the strategic value of AI beyond mere efficiency gains. It's about leveraging AI, particularly generative AI and advanced analytics, to redefine customer journeys, anticipate needs, and create proactive, personalized experiences that were previously impossible. AI professionals must seize this moment to: (1) advocate for holistic system redesigns rather than patchwork fixes; (2) demonstrate AI's capacity to create net-new value and competitive advantage; and (3) educate business stakeholders on the transformative potential that extends far beyond simple automation. Embracing this mindset allows AI initiatives to move from cost-center optimizations to strategic growth drivers, ensuring AI development is aligned with true business transformation rather than just perpetuating legacy inefficiencies.
