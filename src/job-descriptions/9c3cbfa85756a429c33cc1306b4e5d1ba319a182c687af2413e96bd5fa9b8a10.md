**About this opportunity:  
  
**

Machine learning models deployed in practice often encounter data distributions that differ slightly from those on which they were trained. Even mild distribution shifts can lead to increased misclassification rates and reduced reliability. The common remedy, fine-tuning or retraining, is costly in terms of computation, data requirements, and time. There is a need for lightweight approaches that maintain performance under such shifts without requiring immediate retraining of the model.  
  

In this thesis work, the task is to investigate whether conformal prediction (CP) methods can be applied as a correction layer on top of existing models to mitigate misclassification under distribution shift. The objective is to improve prediction reliability and calibration while avoiding the immediate costs of model retraining.  
  

**What you will do:  
  
**

\- Perform a literature survey to gain familiarity with relevant classifiers (e.g. random forests, multi-layer neural networks, etc.), and conformal prediction theory, including conformal test martingales.  
\- Develop methods and a framework for applying conformal prediction as a correction mechanism under distributional drift, positioned as a complementary or even an alternative to online training.  
\- Perform a quantitative evaluation measured in terms of accuracy, coverage, and computational cost, as compared to fine-tuning baselines, showing how performance varies with drift severity.  
\- Demonstrate cost benefits relative to retraining, including reduced carry-on library and maintenance costs for production models.  
\- Documentation of the work.  
\- The thesis will be concluded with a result presentation for the Ericsson team.  
  

**The skills you bring:  
  
**

\- Currently pursuing a master’s degree in an AI/ML-related data science program, or in a related field such as computer engineering, electrical engineering, physics engineering, or similar.  
\- Basic proficiency in computer science and in programming with Python.  
\- Good knowledge of machine learning, probability theory, and statistics.  
  
**Additional detail:  
  
**

Conformal prediction methods are well-established and supported by existing theory and code libraries. Public benchmark datasets will be used. The main contributions will be in implementation, experimental design, and performance analysis, making the work suitable for the scope of 1-2 master’s thesis students.
